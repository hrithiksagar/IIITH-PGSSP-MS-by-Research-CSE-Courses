Independent samples t-test Parametric ▼
What it testsAre the means of two independent groups significantly different?
Assumptions: Normal distribution in both groups, interval data, homogeneity of variances (Levene's test)
Example: Do men and women differ in exam scores? Group A (men) vs Group B (women) — each person tested once. → t(df) = ..., p < .05
Also called: Unrelated t-test, between-subjects t-test
Related (paired) t-test Parametric ▼
What it testsIs there a significant difference in means across two time points or conditions for the same participants?
Assumptions: Differences between pairs are normally distributed, interval data
Example: Same students tested before and after revision. Score at Time 1 vs Time 2. → t(df) = ..., p < .05
Key idea: Subtracts each person's scores — removes individual variation. More powerful than independent t-test.

>2 groups
One-way ANOVA Parametric ▼
What it testsAre there differences in means across 3+ independent groups?
Assumptions: Normality (Shapiro-Wilk), homogeneity of variances (Levene's). If variances unequal → Welch ANOVA
Example: 3 groups each get a different drug. Compare mean recovery times. F(2, 87) = 5.3, p = .007, η² = .11
Post-hoc needed if F is significant — to find WHICH groups differ (Tukey, Bonferroni).
Test statistic: F = MS_between / MS_within
Repeated measures ANOVA Parametric ▼
What it testsDo means differ across 3+ conditions/timepoints measured on the same people?
Assumptions: Normality + Sphericity (Mauchly's test). If violated → Greenhouse-Geisser or Huynh-Feldt correction
Example: Same students tested after re-reading, answering questions, and creating questions. F(2, 10) = 19.09, p < .001, η² = .79
Sphericity: Assumes variances of differences between all condition pairs are equal (the within-subjects version of homogeneity).

(ordinal data)
2 groups
Mann-Whitney U Non-param (ordinal) ▼
What it testsDo two independent groups have different rank distributions? (Non-parametric alternative to independent t-test)
When to use: Data is ordinal, or normality is violated with small samples
Example: Compare pain rankings (1–10 scale) between two treatment groups. Ranks all scores together, compares rank sums.
Also called: Wilcoxon Rank-Sum test. Tests medians, not means.
Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Non-param (ordinal) ▼
What it testsAre there differences between two related conditions in the same people? (Non-parametric alternative to paired t-test)
When to use: Ordinal data, or normality violated in paired design
Example: Participants rate anxiety before and after therapy (ordinal 1–10). Ranks the magnitude of differences, ignores ties.
Difference from sign test: Considers the size of differences, not just direction.

(ordinal data)
>2 groups
Kruskal-Wallis Non-param (ordinal) ▼
What it testsDo 3+ independent groups differ in rank distributions? (Non-parametric alternative to one-way ANOVA)
When to use: Normality violated OR ordinal DV, 3+ groups, different participants
Example: Compare satisfaction ratings across 3 different apps. Ranks all scores, tests if rank sums differ between groups.
Note: Like Mann-Whitney, but for 3+ groups. Still needs post-hoc if significant.
Friedman's ANOVA Non-param (ordinal) ▼
What it testsDo the same participants differ across 3+ conditions? (Non-parametric alternative to repeated measures ANOVA)
When to use: Normality violated in repeated measures design, or ordinal data across 3+ timepoints
Example: Same students scored after re-reading, answering questions, creating questions. Ranks each person's scores within their own row, then compares rank sums across conditions.
Test stat: M = 12/(Nk(k+1)) × ΣR²ᵢ − 3N(k+1)
Non-parametric
(categorical data)
Chi-square (χ²) test Non-param (categorical) ▼
What it testsIs there an association between two categorical variables in independent groups? Are observed frequencies different from expected?
Assumptions: Expected frequency ≥ 5 in each cell, independent observations
Example: Is there a relationship between gender (M/F) and voting choice (A/B/C)? → χ²(2) = 8.3, p = .016, Cramér's V = .21
Chi-square = Σ (O − E)² / E — bigger = more different from expected.
Binomial sign test Non-param (categorical) ▼
What it testsIn a repeated/matched design, do participants systematically score higher in one condition vs another? (Weakest non-param test — only uses direction, not magnitude)
When to use: Categorical outcome or when you can only tell if scores went up or down, not by how much
Example: 20 students tested before/after tutoring. 15 improved, 5 worsened. Is 15/20 significantly above chance (50%)? Uses binomial distribution.
Weaker than Wilcoxon: Wilcoxon uses the size of differences; sign test only uses +/−. Use sign test when you can't rank differences.
