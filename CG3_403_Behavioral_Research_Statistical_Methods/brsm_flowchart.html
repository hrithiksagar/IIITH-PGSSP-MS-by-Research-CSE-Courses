Page 2 of 3 — Parametric & Non-parametric Tests: Definitions, Formulas & Examples
Parametric Tests
Independent t-test Parametric
DefinitionTests whether the means of two independent, unrelated groups differ significantly. Assumes normality within each group and homogeneity of variance.
When to useTwo separate groups; continuous interval/ratio DV; parametric assumptions met.
Formulat = (M₁ − M₂) / SE_diff  ·  df = n₁ + n₂ − 2
Effect sizeCohen's d = (M₁−M₂)/SD_pooled. Trivial <0.1, small 0.1–0.3, moderate 0.3–0.5, large >0.5.
Example: Do male and female students differ in exam scores? IV = gender (2 levels), DV = score (continuous).
Paired (Related) t-test Parametric
DefinitionCompares means from the same participants at two timepoints, or from matched pairs. Operates on the difference scores d = X₁ − X₂.
When to useWithin-subjects or matched pairs design. Normality of difference scores required.
Formulat = M_d / (SD_d / √n)  ·  df = n − 1
Effect sizeCohen's d = M_d / SD_d.
Example: Do knowledge scores improve after a group discussion? Pre-test M=83, post-test M=84, n=1000. Significant statistically but d is tiny — always report effect size!
One-way ANOVA Parametric
DefinitionTests whether at least one group mean differs among ≥3 independent groups. Partitions total variance into between-group (SS_b) and within-group (SS_w) components.
AssumptionsNormality (Shapiro-Wilk); homogeneity of variance (Levene's). Robust for n > 25/group. If largest variance < 4–5× smallest, ANOVA results remain valid.
FormulaF = MS_between / MS_within  ·  df_b = G−1, df_w = N−G
Post-hocOnly if F is significant: Tukey HSD, Bonferroni, or Games-Howell (unequal n/var).
Effect sizeη² = SS_between / SS_total. Small: 0.01, medium: 0.06, large: 0.14.
Example: Do three teaching methods (lecture, flipped, online) produce different exam averages? IV = method (3 levels), DV = score.
Repeated Measures ANOVA Parametric
DefinitionANOVA for within-subjects designs: same participants measured ≥3 times. Removes individual differences from error variance → higher power than between-subjects ANOVA.
Key assumptionSphericity — variances of all pairwise difference scores must be equal. Test: Mauchly's W. If violated: Greenhouse-Geisser or Huynh-Feldt epsilon correction.
Effect sizePartial η² or generalised η².
Example: Anxiety scores at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks of therapy — same participants. Check sphericity before reporting F.
Welch ANOVA Parametric
DefinitionRobust alternative to one-way ANOVA that does not assume equal variances. Uses weighted group means. Recommended when Levene's test is significant.
Post-hocGames-Howell: does not assume equal variances or equal sample sizes.
Example: Comparing salaries across 4 departments where one department has much higher variance than others → Welch ANOVA + Games-Howell.
MANOVA Parametric
DefinitionMultivariate ANOVA: tests group differences on ≥2 DVs simultaneously. Controls family-wise Type I error across DVs. Requires multivariate normality.
StatisticsWilks' Λ (most common), Pillai's trace (most robust), Hotelling's T², Roy's largest root.
Follow-upIf MANOVA is significant, run individual ANOVAs per DV with Bonferroni correction.
Example: Do three therapy types differ on both anxiety AND depression simultaneously? One MANOVA instead of two separate ANOVAs.
Non-parametric Tests
Mann-Whitney U Test Non-param
DefinitionNon-parametric equivalent of the independent t-test. Combines and ranks all scores from both groups; tests whether one group's ranks are systematically higher (i.e., different medians). Between-subjects design, skewed distributions, ordinal data.
AssumptionA real difference between two populations should cause one sample's scores to be generally larger. If combined and ranked, larger ranks concentrate in one group.
H₀The two populations share an identical distribution (same median).
Effect sizer = Z / √N
Example: Pain ratings (1–10 Likert, skewed) for Drug A vs Drug B. Non-normal → Mann-Whitney U instead of independent t-test.
Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test Non-param
DefinitionNon-parametric equivalent of the paired t-test. Computes difference scores, ranks their absolute values, then compares the sum of positive vs negative ranks. Within-subjects or matched pairs design.
H₀Population median difference = 0. Reject H₀ if observed W ≤ critical W value.
Data levelOrdinal (rank order). Also valid for discrete related samples where parametric assumptions are violated.
Example: Did students perform better in mock exam vs final GRE? H₀: med diff = 0; H₁: med diff > 0 (1-tailed). If paired differences are non-normal → Wilcoxon SR.
Kruskal-Wallis Test Non-param
DefinitionNon-parametric equivalent of one-way ANOVA. All scores from ≥3 independent groups are pooled and ranked; tests if rank sums differ significantly between groups.
When to use≥3 independent groups; ordinal DV or violated normality; use when ANOVA assumptions fail.
FormulaH = [12 / N(N+1)] × Σ(R²ₖ / nₖ) − 3(N+1)
Example: Job satisfaction (ordinal 1–5) across 3 departments. Non-normal data → Kruskal-Wallis (H statistic) instead of one-way ANOVA.
Friedman's ANOVA Non-param
DefinitionNon-parametric equivalent of repeated measures ANOVA. Ranks scores within each participant (block) across ≥3 conditions, then compares rank sums across conditions.
FormulaM = [12 / Nk(k+1)] × ΣR²ᵢ − 3N(k+1)
Wherek = number of conditions, N = number of participants, Rᵢ = sum of ranks for condition i.
Example: 6 students rate 3 study strategies. Ranks within each student: Reread=1, Answer Qs=2, Create Qs=3. Friedman M=9.33, df=2, p=.009 → strategies differ significantly.
Chi-square Independence Categorical
DefinitionTests whether two categorical variables are independent. Compares observed frequencies (O) to expected frequencies (E) assuming independence.
AssumptionsExpected frequency ≥ 5 in each cell. df = (rows−1)(cols−1).
Formulaχ² = Σ (O − E)² / E  ·  E = (row total × col total) / N
Effect size2×2: φ = √(χ²/n). Larger: Cramér's V = √(χ²/n·df*). Small=0.1, medium=0.3, large=0.5.
Example: Extroverts vs introverts — comfortable with public speaking? Extroverts: 10 comfortable / 40 not. Introverts: 40/10. χ²(2, n=150)=8.23, p<.05, V=0.23 → moderate effect.
Binomial Sign Test Categorical
DefinitionTests whether the proportion of positive change signs in a within-subjects design differs from 50% (chance). Uses the binomial distribution. The simplest non-parametric test for related pairs.
H₀p(positive change) = 0.5. Count positives (S); compare to binomial critical value.
When to useOnly sign of change is available (not magnitude). Related pairs or matched design.
Example: After diet intervention, 18 of 20 participants lost weight (+). p(S≥18 | p=0.5) = 0.0002 → reject H₀, diet had a significant positive effect.

Regression & GLM
Simple Linear Regression Regression
DefinitionModels the linear relationship between one continuous predictor (X) and one continuous outcome (Y). Uses Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) to find the line minimising the sum of squared residuals (SSR).
ModelY = b₀ + b₁X + ε
Key outputR² (proportion of variance explained), F-statistic (overall model), β₁ (slope = change in Y per 1-unit increase in X), p-values. Note: for 1 predictor, R² = r² (Pearson).
AssumptionsLinearity, normality of residuals, homoscedasticity, independence of errors. Check with residual plots and Q-Q plots.
Example (housing.csv): Predict median_house_value from median_income alone. β₁ = slope ($ per unit income); R² = how much income explains price variation.
Multiple Linear Regression Regression
DefinitionExtends simple regression to ≥2 predictors. Each β = unique contribution of that predictor holding all others constant (partial regression coefficient). Adjusted R² penalises for adding extra predictors.
ModelY = b₀ + b₁X₁ + b₂X₂ + … + bₙXₙ + ε
MulticollinearityVIF > 10 (or > 5 strict): predictors too correlated; coefficients become unstable and uninterpretable. Solutions: remove redundant predictor, combine them, or run PCA/FA first to create orthogonal components.
Example (housing.csv): Predict house value from income, housing_median_age, total_rooms, population, and ocean_proximity simultaneously. Check each predictor's unique contribution via standardised β.
Logistic Regression GLM
DefinitionModels the probability of a binary outcome (0/1) using a logistic link function (sigmoid). Estimates coefficients via Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), not OLS. Coefficients are log-odds.
Modellog(p / 1−p) = b₀ + b₁X₁ + … + bₙXₙ
Interpretationexp(β) = odds ratio. OR > 1: predictor increases odds of outcome. 1-unit increase in X multiplies odds by exp(β). Model significance: χ² likelihood ratio test.
OutputOdds ratios, Wald statistic per predictor, Nagelkerke R², classification accuracy (confusion matrix), AUC-ROC.
Example (binary.csv): Predict admission (0/1) from GRE, GPA, and rank. Each 1-point GRE increase multiplies odds of admission by exp(β_gre). Higher GPA and lower rank → higher admission probability.
PCA & Factor Analysis Dimension Reduction
PCATransforms correlated variables into orthogonal (uncorrelated) principal components ordered by variance explained. Data-driven. No theory assumed. Use scree plot or Kaiser rule (eigenvalue > 1) to select components. Components are linear combinations of original variables.
Factor AnalysisIdentifies latent factors explaining shared (common) variance among observed variables. Theory-driven — you hypothesise what the factors represent. Rotation improves interpretability: varimax (orthogonal, uncorrelated factors) or oblimin (correlated factors allowed).
Key differencePCA explains total variance; FA explains only common variance. PCA = preprocessing tool for dimensionality reduction. FA = measurement model for latent constructs (e.g., psychological scales).
Example: 20-item personality questionnaire → FA reveals 5 latent factors (openness, conscientiousness…). Use factor scores as DVs in ANOVA. Alternatively, PCA on housing.csv variables to reduce multicollinearity before regression.
Association Tests
Pearson's r Parametric
DefinitionMeasures strength and direction of the linear relationship between two continuous variables. Ranges −1 to +1. 0 = no linear relationship. Association only — does not imply causation.
AssumptionsBoth variables approximately normally distributed; linear relationship (check scatterplot — Anscombe's quartet!); no extreme outliers.
Formular = Σ[(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)] / [(n−1)·SD_x·SD_y]
Effect sizes|r|: small=0.1, medium=0.3, large=0.5. r² = proportion of shared variance. For 1-predictor regression: r² = R².
Example: Is there a linear relationship between hours studied and exam score? r = 0.62 → moderate-large positive relationship. r² = 0.38 → studying explains 38% of score variance.
Spearman's ρ Non-parametric
DefinitionNon-parametric equivalent of Pearson's r. Computes Pearson correlation on rank-transformed data. Measures the strength of monotonic (not necessarily linear) association. Robust to outliers.
When to useNon-normal distributions, ordinal variables, or when outliers distort Pearson r. Use if scatterplot shows monotonic but non-linear trend.
Formulaρ = 1 − [6Σd²ᵢ / n(n²−1)]   (d = rank difference)
Example: Class rank (ordinal) vs anxiety level (ordinal Likert). Both ordinal → Spearman's ρ. Or: latitude vs temperature — skewed distribution → Spearman more appropriate than Pearson.
Fisher's Exact Test Categorical
DefinitionCalculates the exact probability of observing the given 2×2 contingency table (or more extreme tables) given fixed marginal totals, using the hypergeometric distribution. Valid for any sample size — no approximation.
When to use2×2 tables where expected cell frequency < 5 in any cell, or total n < 20. Use instead of chi-square when its assumptions are violated.
H₀The two categorical variables are independent (same H₀ as chi-square).
Example: 12 patients: 3 on Drug A (2 recovered, 1 not), 9 on Drug B (3 recovered, 6 not). Small n → chi-square assumptions violated → Fisher's exact p = 0.54 → no significant difference.
Chi-square Goodness-of-fit Categorical
DefinitionTests whether a single categorical variable's observed frequencies match a hypothesised (expected) distribution. Unlike the independence test, only one variable is involved.
Formulaχ² = Σ (O − E)² / E  ·  df = k − 1
Example: Are 100 cards dealt equally across 4 suits (expected: 25 each)? Observed: ♣20, ♥30, ♦25, ♠25. χ²(3) = (25+25+0+0)/25 = 2.0, p = .57 → no significant deviation from equal distribution.
Corrections, Bayesian Inference & Hypothesis Testing Logic
Multiple Comparisons Corrections Caution
ProblemRunning k tests at α=0.05 inflates family-wise Type I error to 1−(0.95)^k. At k=14 tests this exceeds 50% — you're more likely than not to get at least one false positive by chance alone.
Bonferroniα_corrected = α / k. Very conservative. Best when k is small and all comparisons are planned. e.g., 20 tests → threshold p < 0.0025 per test.
Holm-BonferroniStep-down: rank p-values smallest to largest; apply sequentially tightened thresholds (α/k, α/(k−1), …). Less conservative than Bonferroni while still controlling FWER.
FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg)Controls expected proportion of false discoveries (not FWER). Less conservative; preferred in exploratory genomic or neuroimaging studies with hundreds of tests.
Example: Post-hoc comparisons after significant ANOVA with 4 groups = 6 pairwise tests. Bonferroni threshold: p < 0.05/6 = 0.0083 per comparison.
Bayesian Inference Framework
DefinitionUpdates prior beliefs with observed data (likelihood) to produce a posterior distribution over parameters. All conclusions are probabilistic statements about parameters, not about data given a null.
FormulaP(θ | data) ∝ P(data | θ) × P(θ)
Bayes Factor BF₁₀Ratio of marginal likelihoods. BF > 3: moderate evidence for H₁. BF > 10: strong. BF < 1/3: evidence for H₀. BF = 1: no evidence either way. No arbitrary threshold unlike p < 0.05.
Credible intervalsA 95% CrI means there is 95% probability the true parameter lies within it — more intuitive than frequentist confidence intervals (which do not mean this).
Bayesian paired t-testReplaces frequentist paired t-test. Reports BF₁₀ and posterior of mean difference instead of t and p-value.
Example: Bayesian t-test for CBT vs control on anxiety: BF₁₀ = 12.4 → strong evidence CBT reduces anxiety. Can also quantify evidence for the null (H₀), unlike NHST which can only reject it.
